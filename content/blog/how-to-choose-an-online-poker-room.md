## The decision matters more than it seems

Which room you play on affects your win rate before you've played a single hand — through rake, game selection, and software compatibility. It's worth a genuine look rather than defaulting to whichever site a friend mentioned.

## Game selection and traffic

A room's *player pool* determines how soft or tough your games actually are, independent of your own skill. More traffic generally means more tables running at your preferred stake and format, less time spent waiting for a seat, and — often more importantly — a wider mix of recreational players rather than a pool dominated by regulars grinding the same stakes you are.

## Rake structure

Rake eats into your win rate on every single pot, so it's worth understanding a room's specific structure — the percentage, the cap, and whether it scales sensibly with stakes — before committing serious volume there. See [what rake is and how it actually affects your win rate](/blog/what-is-rake-in-online-poker) for what to actually look at.

## Rakeback and promotions

A room with slightly higher posted rake but a strong rakeback or VIP program can net out better than a lower-rake room with nothing back, especially for higher-volume players. See [what rakeback is and how it affects your bankroll](/blog/rakeback-and-your-bankroll) for how to actually compare offers on equal footing rather than by headline percentage alone.

## Software and tracker compatibility

If you use a HUD or track your results — and if you're reading this, you probably do or should — check whether the room's hand history format is one your tracker reads natively. Some rooms export in a PokerStars-style format trackers already understand out of the box; others, like CoinPoker, use their own format that needs converting first. That's not a reason to avoid a room, just something to plan for — [PokerHandsConverter](/) does that conversion if that's the room you've landed on.

## Table formats and stakes offered

Check that the room actually spreads the format you want to play at meaningful volume — some rooms are deep at 6-max cash but thin on full ring, or strong on tournaments but light on cash traffic outside peak hours. See [6-max vs full ring cash games](/blog/6max-vs-full-ring-cash-games) if you haven't settled on a primary format yet.

## Security and reputation

Basic diligence still applies: how long the room has operated, how withdrawals are handled, and general reputation among other players are worth a quick check before depositing anything meaningful — the same due diligence you'd apply to any platform holding your money.
