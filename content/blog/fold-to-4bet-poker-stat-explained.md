## What fold to 4-bet measures

**Fold to 4-bet** is how often a player folds after they 3-bet and then face a 4-bet. It is the other side of the [4-bet%](/blog/4-bet-percentage-poker-stat-explained): one stat tells you how often someone 4-bets, and this one tells you how they respond when they get 4-bet.

```
fold to 4-bet% = times you folded to a 4-bet after 3-betting / times you faced a 4-bet after 3-betting
```

It only counts hands where you 3-bet first and then got 4-bet, so it comes from a small number of hands.

## A worked example

You 3-bet in a number of spots and got 4-bet 12 times. You folded 8 and continued 4. Your fold to 4-bet is 8 / 12, about 67%.

## The break-even math for a 4-bet bluff

Suppose blinds are 0.5 and 1, you open to 3 big blinds, your opponent 3-bets to 9, and you 4-bet to 21 as a pure bluff that has no chance when called. You risk another 18 big blinds to win 10.5 (their 9 plus the 1.5 of blinds). The bluff breaks even when they fold often enough:

```
fold% needed = 18 / (18 + 10.5) = about 63%
```

If their fold to 4-bet is above that, a pure bluff is profitable. In practice, hands with some equity when called (like a suited ace) need a bit less. The numbers change with your sizes, so recalculate for the sizes you actually use. This ignores rake and assumes you give up if they continue.

## What a normal number looks like

As a rough guide, many players fold to a 4-bet more than half the time, and recreational players often fold even more. Strong players who defend widely may fold much less. It varies a lot with position and stack size.

## Reading a high or low number

- **High fold to 4-bet (above the break-even).** Their 3-bets are often bluffs they give up. 4-bet them lighter, including bluffs.
- **Low fold to 4-bet.** They continue with a wide range. Tighten your 4-bet bluffing and 4-bet mostly for value.
- **Compare it to their 3-bet%.** A very high 3-bet% with a high fold to 4-bet is the classic exploitable player: they pressure you, but fold when you push back.

## Using it on yourself

If you fold to 4-bets almost every time, good opponents will 3-bet you and 4-bet bluff you without risk. If you never fold, you will lose a lot against the very strong 4-bet range. Look at your hands that continued and ask whether you were calling with hands that have enough equity. See [fold to 3-bet](/blog/fold-to-3bet-poker-stat-explained) for the same idea one step earlier.

## One caution

You face 4-bets so rarely that this stat needs a very large sample. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) shows why a handful of 4-bets proves nothing.
