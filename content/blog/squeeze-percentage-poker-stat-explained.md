## What a squeeze is

A **squeeze** is a 3-bet made after one player has opened and at least one other player has called. The raiser and the caller are both "squeezed": the caller's money is already in, and the pot is big and multiway, so the 3-bet puts a lot of pressure on both.

```
squeeze% = times you 3-bet after a raise and a call / times you had that chance
```

The chance exists when someone raised before you, at least one player called, and it was your turn to act. It is different from a regular 3-bet (see [VPIP, PFR and 3-bet](/blog/understanding-vpip-pfr-3bet-hud-stats)), because the extra dead money from the caller changes what the play needs to win.

## A worked example

You are on the button. A player opens to 3 big blinds, another player calls, and you can squeeze. In a typical game, the pot is about 7.5 big blinds before you act (3 + 3 + the blinds). If you 3-bet to about 13 big blinds, you only need the raiser and the caller to fold often enough to make it work, and the dead money gives you a lot of room. Across 60 spots you squeezed 4 times, so your squeeze% is 4 / 60, about 7%.

## Why the squeeze is so effective

- **More dead money.** The caller's chips and the opener's chips are in the pot, so a bluff needs to work less often than a normal 3-bet.
- **The caller is capped.** A player who just called usually does not have the strongest hands, because those would often 3-bet. That makes a squeeze hard to continue against.
- **The opener is stuck in the middle.** With a player still to act behind them, the opener often has to fold marginal hands.

## What a normal number looks like

Squeeze spots are rare, so numbers are noisy. As a rough guide, many winning regulars squeeze somewhere in the mid-single digits to around ten percent of their chances, with more in late position. Treat that as a direction rather than a target.

## Reading a high or low number

- **A high squeeze%.** A player who squeezes a lot has a wide range with bluffs. Against them, 4-bet more with your strong hands and call wider.
- **A low squeeze%.** A player who almost never squeezes has mostly strong hands when they do. You can open and call with less fear, and fold more often when they finally 3-bet.

## Using it on yourself

If your squeeze% is near zero, you are missing a very profitable play, especially from the button and the blinds against a late-position opener and a loose caller. Choose a mix of strong hands and some bluffs with good blockers, such as suited aces. Pair it with your [fold to 3-bet](/blog/fold-to-3bet-poker-stat-explained) and your [4-bet%](/blog/4-bet-percentage-poker-stat-explained) to plan how you respond when someone fights back.

## One caution

Because squeeze chances are rare, you need many thousands of hands before a squeeze% is meaningful. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) explains why.
