## What a donk bet is

A **donk bet** (or lead) is a bet on the flop made by a player who called before the flop, into the player who raised. Normally the preflop raiser bets first on the flop because they have the initiative. When the caller bets first instead, that is a donk bet.

```
donk bet% = times you bet the flop first as a caller / times you had the chance
```

The chance exists when you called a raise before the flop, you are first to act on the flop, and the raiser has not yet acted. Many trackers show a flop donk bet% and, separately, turn and river versions.

## A worked example

You call a preflop raise from the big blind and see 50 flops as the first player to act. You bet first in 4 of them and check the other 46. Your flop donk bet% is 4 / 50 = 8%.

## What a normal number looks like

Strong players donk rarely. A rough guide is that many winning regulars lead the flop in single-digit percentages, often much lower, because checking to the raiser and then using a check-raise protects a wide range of hands. Players who donk much more often than that are usually less experienced, or are using it as a specific plan on certain boards.

There are good reasons to lead on some flops, such as boards that strongly favour the caller's range (for example low, connected cards that hit the big blind's calling range). But as a stat, a high donk bet% usually separates recreational players from regulars.

## Reading a donk bet

- **Against a player with a very low donk%.** When a regular who almost never leads suddenly bets, take it seriously. Their range is usually a strong hand or a draw they want to protect.
- **Against a player with a high donk%.** Their range is wide and often weak: small stabs with marginal pairs, draws, and bluffs. Raise more, and do not fold hands that are comfortable calling.
- **Bet size matters.** Small leads often come from medium-strength hands that want information or protection. Large leads are polarised towards very strong hands and bluffs.

## Using it on yourself

If you never donk, make sure you are using the check-raise well, because those are your main tools out of position. See [check-raise percentage](/blog/check-raise-percentage-poker-stat-explained). If you donk a lot, check whether the results justify it by comparing the hands where you lead with the ones where you check and call. Often the answer is to lead less and check-raise more.

## One caution

Donk bets are rare spots, so a donk bet% can look very high or very low on a small sample. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) shows how many opportunities you need before the number means anything.
