## Rake, in one sentence

Rake is the fee a poker room takes out of the game for hosting it — the house's cut, taken either from cash pots or as a tournament entry fee on top of the prize pool. It's not unique to any one site; every real-money poker room charges it in some form. What varies a lot is how it's structured, and how much it actually costs you over time.

## How cash game rake is usually structured

Most online cash games use **percentage rake**, taken out of the pot at showdown (or when the pot is won), up to a **cap** — a maximum rake amount regardless of pot size. A common shape is something like 5% up to a cap that scales with stakes and table size. Some rooms instead use **time-based rake**, charging players a flat fee per half-hour seated rather than a cut of each pot — less common online than live, but it exists.

## Tournament rake (the fee)

In tournaments, rake shows up as the "fee" portion of a buy-in — a $10+1 tournament has a $1 fee on top of the $10 that goes to the prize pool. It's usually a fixed percentage of the buy-in, commonly somewhere around 10%, though this varies by site and tournament size.

## Why rake matters more than a few cents a hand suggests

Rake is small on any single hand, but it compounds relentlessly across thousands of hands a serious player sees. At low stakes especially, rake can be a significant fraction of the edge a skilled player has over the field — which is exactly why "rake-adjusted win rate" is a more honest number than raw win rate when comparing your results across different games, stakes, or sites with different rake structures.

## Rake isn't always just "rake"

Some rooms add extra fee line items beyond the standard rake — CoinPoker's Splash Fee is one example, a separate charge on top of rake that shows up on certain cash tables. That extra fee is a common cause of ["Invalid Pot Size" import errors](/blog/fix-invalid-pot-size-error-coinpoker-import) in tracking software that isn't expecting it.

## Getting some of it back

Rake isn't necessarily a fixed cost you just absorb — many rooms return a portion of it to players through rakeback or a VIP/rewards program. See [what rakeback is and how it affects your bankroll](/blog/rakeback-and-your-bankroll) for how that works and why it matters more the more volume you play.

## Rake structure is a real factor in picking a room

Because rake compounds so heavily over volume, it's worth weighing alongside traffic, software and rakeback when you're deciding where to actually play — see [how to choose an online poker room](/blog/how-to-choose-an-online-poker-room) for the full checklist.
