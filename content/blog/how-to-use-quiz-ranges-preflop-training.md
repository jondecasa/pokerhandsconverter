Reading a preflop chart is easy. Remembering it at the table, with a timer running and a hand you have never seen in that spot, is the hard part. **Quiz Ranges** exists for that gap: it takes the charts from the Ranges section and turns them into quick questions, so you practise *deciding* instead of just *looking*.

This guide walks through a full round, explains what the result screen is telling you and ends with a routine you can repeat in ten minutes a day.

## What you need

Quiz Ranges is part of the subscriber area, like the Ranges section it is built on. Open **Quiz Ranges** in the left menu. If you have not looked at the charts yet, spend a few minutes in **Ranges** first: the quiz only asks about the studies published there, and the correct answer is always what the chart says.

## Step 1: Pick a study

The first page lists the available studies, each with the number of spot types in its pool. Click a card to start. A study that is not ready for the quiz shows as "not available yet".

Some charts are left out of the pool on purpose. Charts that only *classify* hands (for example which hands are favourable or unfavourable in a spot) do not prescribe an action, so there is nothing to answer and they never appear.

## Step 2: Answer the spot

Every round has **20 random spots**. For each one you see:

- Three small tags at the top: the **situation** (for example an open-raise chart or a defence against a 3-bet), the **position** and the **stack depth or button** the chart belongs to.
- Your two **hole cards**, drawn as cards.
- The **answer buttons**. They are the actions of that chart's own legend, with the same colours you saw in the Ranges section, such as *OR*, *Call* or *4-bet*.

Pick the action your chart prescribes. You can click, or press the **number keys** (1, 2, 3…) shown on each button. You get the verdict immediately, and if you missed, the right action is shown. Press **Enter** (or the button) to move on.

### Folding is an answer too

A hand the chart leaves unpainted is a **fold**. If the chart has no Fold button in its legend, one is added, so folding is always possible. This matters: about three in ten questions are hands outside the range. Players who only study the painted cells tend to over-play the edge of the range, and this is how the quiz catches it.

### Rare actions still show up

Hands are drawn action by action, not purely at random. Otherwise a chart with a thin 4-bet range would almost never ask you about a 4-bet, and that is exactly the spot you want to rehearse.

## Step 3: Read the result

At the end you get your score out of 20 and then three blocks:

1. **Where you missed the most.** The situation group with the most misses, for example "vs 3-bet". This is your next topic of study.
2. **By situation.** A bar per group with the score and percentage. Green bars are fine; short bars show where to look.
3. **Review your mistakes.** Every miss, with your answer next to the correct one and the position it belonged to.

A perfect round shows a green "no mistakes" message instead of the weakest group.

## Step 4: Fix, then repeat

Press **New quiz** for a fresh set of 20 spots, **Choose another study** to change chart, or **Review the ranges** to open the charts again.

Do not read the result as a grade. Read it as a to-do list.

## A simple routine

- **Start narrow.** Take one study and play rounds until the weakest group stops being the same one twice in a row.
- **Go back to the chart for the misses only.** Open the Ranges section, find the spot from your mistakes list and look at the shape of the range, not each hand. Edges are usually patterns: suited connectors stop at a certain gap, offsuit broadways stop at a certain rank.
- **Re-run the quiz the next day.** Memory you can reproduce after a night is worth more than a perfect score right after studying.
- **Combine it with your real hands.** Once your results hold above 90%, check them against your own play: the same stats you track in [VPIP, PFR and 3-bet](/blog/understanding-vpip-pfr-3bet-hud-stats) or your [4-bet%](/blog/4-bet-percentage-poker-stat-explained) should start to match the charts you are drilling.

## What the quiz does not do

It does not save a history, so it will not show your progress across days: keep your own note if you want to track it. And it tests the charts as they are written; it cannot tell you whether a chart is right for your table, since opponents who fold too much or call too much change the best play. Treat the charts as your baseline and the quiz as the way to make that baseline automatic.
