## Why bankroll management matters more online than live

Online, you can play multiple tables at once, move up and down in stakes in seconds, and rack up a statistically meaningful sample of hands in a single sitting. That's an advantage for improving quickly — and a real risk for going broke quickly if your bankroll isn't sized for the swings that come with it.

## Think in buy-ins, not dollars

The number that matters isn't your bankroll in dollars — it's how many buy-ins deep it is for the stake you're playing. $500 is a healthy cushion at one stake and reckless at another. Reasoning in buy-ins keeps the same rule applicable as you move up or down.

## Rough starting guidelines

For online No-Limit Hold'em cash games, a commonly cited range is **20–40 buy-ins** for a full stake, with the lower end fitting a tighter, lower-variance style and the higher end fitting a looser, more aggressive one (or someone who multi-tables heavily, which increases variance per hour even if it doesn't change variance per hand). These are starting points, not rules carved in stone — your own risk tolerance and how bad a losing stretch you can emotionally absorb without playing worse matter just as much as the math.

Tournaments generally call for a deeper bankroll in buy-ins than cash, since a single tournament's outcome is far more binary (min-cash or bust, roughly) — 50-100+ buy-ins is a common range for regular tournament grinders, wider than cash for exactly that reason.

## Shot-taking and moving down

Bankroll management isn't only about avoiding going broke — it's a framework for two specific decisions:

**Taking a shot at a higher stake** — usually justified once your bankroll comfortably clears the buy-in threshold for that stake, not the moment you technically have enough for one or two buy-ins.

**Moving back down** — the harder discipline. If a downswing eats into your bankroll enough that you're now under-rolled for your current stake, moving down isn't an admission of failure, it's the same math working in the other direction.

## Why this connects to your tracked stats

Your actual win rate takes a long time to stabilize — see [how many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) — which means you generally can't lean on "my real win rate is X" to justify a bankroll size early on. Sizing conservatively while your sample builds is the safer default. And since variance itself differs by game format, see [6-max vs full ring cash games](/blog/6max-vs-full-ring-cash-games) for how the swings compare between the two.
