## How to read a bankroll chart

Bankroll rules are written in **buy-ins**: how many full buy-ins you hold for the stake you play. A chart just multiplies the buy-in by that number. The rule behind it is in [bankroll management for online cash game players](/blog/bankroll-management-online-cash-games): a commonly cited range is 20 to 40 buy-ins for cash games, with the lower end for a tighter, lower-variance style and the higher end for a looser, more aggressive one.

```
bankroll needed = buy-in x number of buy-ins you want to hold
```

## Cash games (100 big blind buy-in)

| Stake | Buy-in | 20 buy-ins | 30 buy-ins | 40 buy-ins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NL10 | $10 | $200 | $300 | $400 |
| NL25 | $25 | $500 | $750 | $1,000 |
| NL50 | $50 | $1,000 | $1,500 | $2,000 |
| NL100 | $100 | $2,000 | $3,000 | $4,000 |
| NL200 | $200 | $4,000 | $6,000 | $8,000 |

Use the left columns if you are tight, play fewer tables and have a stable win rate. Use the right columns if you are aggressive, multi-table a lot, or have a small sample of hands.

## Tournaments

Tournament results are far more binary: a few deep runs produce most of the profit, and long losing streaks are normal. Tournament grinders usually hold a bigger bankroll in buy-ins than cash players, commonly 50 to 100 or more.

| Average buy-in | 50 buy-ins | 100 buy-ins |
|---|---|---|
| $5 | $250 | $500 |
| $10 | $500 | $1,000 |
| $20 | $1,000 | $2,000 |
| $50 | $2,500 | $5,000 |
| $100 | $5,000 | $10,000 |

Bigger fields and more top-heavy payouts need more buy-ins. See [poker tournament payouts](/blog/poker-tournament-payouts-and-icm-explained) and your [tournament ROI](/blog/poker-tournament-roi-explained) for how to judge your results.

## When to move up and when to move down

Chart numbers are starting points. A simple way to use them:

- **Move up** only when your bankroll comfortably clears the next stake's number, not as soon as you can afford one buy-in.
- **Move down** when a downswing leaves you under the minimum for your current stake. Moving down is the same math working in the other direction.
- **Treat a shot as a limited experiment.** Decide in advance how many buy-ins you will risk at the higher stake before you go back.

## What the chart does not tell you

A chart assumes you win. A losing or break-even player needs a different plan: improving first, or moving down until the results are positive. The chart also ignores [variance](/blog/poker-variance-standard-deviation-explained): the bigger the swings in your game, the more buy-ins you need. Your [win rate in bb/100](/blog/bb-per-100-poker-win-rate-explained) takes tens of thousands of hands to settle, so size the bankroll conservatively while the sample is small. If you play live, the numbers are different, so see [live poker bankroll management](/blog/live-poker-bankroll-management).

This is an educational guide to a risk framework, not financial advice, and no bankroll rule can guarantee you will not go broke.
