## Why live bankroll management is different

The buy-in rule works the same way live as online: you count how many buy-ins you hold for the stake you play. But live poker changes several inputs, and each one affects how big your bankroll should be.

## Fewer hands per hour

Online, a multi-tabler can play several hundred hands an hour. A live table deals only about 25 to 30 hands per hour. That means your results arrive far more slowly and your sample builds very slowly.

## A worked example

You play 25 hours a week at about 27 hands per hour, which is roughly 675 hands a week. A sample of 100,000 hands, a size at which [win rates start to settle](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats), would take about 148 weeks, close to three years. Until then you cannot trust your measured win rate, so you should lean on conservative bankroll rules rather than "my results say I win X".

## Costs that online players do not have

- **The rake or time charge.** Live rooms often take a high rake per pot or a charge per half hour, which weighs heavily on small stakes. See [what rake is](/blog/what-is-rake-in-online-poker) for how rake works.
- **Tips.** Dealer tips are a real cost per hour.
- **Travel and food.** Getting to the cardroom and playing a long session costs money and time.
- **Your time.** Sessions are long and results per hour are lower than online, so your hourly win rate in big blinds per hour is what matters.

Count these costs as part of your real win rate, not as an afterthought.

## How many buy-ins

A common starting point is the same 20 to 40 buy-ins used for [online cash games](/blog/poker-bankroll-management-chart), but many live players choose the higher end, or more, for three reasons: the samples are small, the rake and tips reduce your edge, and live games often have large stacks and high variance from wild opponents. Treat these as starting points, not rules.

A buy-in in a $1/$2 game is often $200, so 40 buy-ins is $8,000. Adjust it to the stakes and the buy-in limits at your cardroom.

## Keep your poker money separate

Live play often runs on cash, which makes it easy to mix poker money with living expenses. Keep a bankroll that you track, separate from rent and bills, and write down every session: stake, hours, buy-in, cash-out. Without that record you will not know whether you are winning.

## Managing the emotional side

Because results arrive slowly, long downswings feel worse. The math of [variance](/blog/poker-variance-standard-deviation-explained) says they are normal, and [tilt and downswings](/blog/poker-tilt-and-downswings) covers how to keep playing well through them.

## One caution

This is an educational guide to a risk framework, not financial advice. No rule guarantees you will not lose your bankroll.
