## What a c-bet is

A **continuation bet** (c-bet) is a bet on the flop made by the player who raised before the flop, regardless of whether the flop helped them. It's the natural follow-up to preflop aggression.

**Fold to c-bet** is the other side of that action. It measures how often a player folds when they face a continuation bet.

## The formula

```
fold to flop c-bet = times folded to a flop c-bet / times faced a flop c-bet
```

Say an opponent faced your c-bet 20 times and folded 11. Their fold to flop c-bet is 11 / 20 = 55%.

Trackers also record this for the turn (fold to turn c-bet), which tends to behave differently from the flop.

## How to read the number

- **High fold to c-bet.** The opponent gives up too easily. You can c-bet more often, including with weak hands and even air.
- **Low fold to c-bet.** The opponent calls or raises a lot. C-bet less often as a bluff and bet more for value.
- **Around the middle.** Many 6-max players fold to a flop c-bet somewhere around half the time. That's a rough guide: the right number depends on stakes, position and board texture.

## Context changes the stat

- **Position.** A player in position is less likely to fold than one out of position.
- **Heads-up vs multiway.** A multiway c-bet should get more folds than a heads-up c-bet.
- **Board.** Dry boards get c-bet more and folded to more often than wet boards.

A single overall number hides all of this. If your tracker splits it by position, look at the split.

## Using it against opponents

If you can see an opponent's fold to c-bet, you can act on it:

1. Against a **high** fold to c-bet, c-bet wide on the flop, especially against a single caller.
2. Against a **low** fold to c-bet, don't bluff the flop without a plan for the turn, and value bet your good hands.
3. Compare with their **fold to turn c-bet**. A player who calls the flop and folds the turn is a target for a second barrel.

## Use it on yourself too

Your own fold to c-bet shows whether you're being exploited. If it's very high, opponents can bet any flop against you and win, and if it's very low you may be calling too much. ## One caution

Like every situational stat, fold to c-bet needs a decent sample, because it only counts the hands where the opponent faced a c-bet. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) explains how much to trust a number built on a few dozen opportunities.
