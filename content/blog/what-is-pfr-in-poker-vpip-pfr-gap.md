## PFR meaning

**PFR** stands for *preflop raise*. It is the percentage of hands in which a player raised before the flop. It is the aggression partner of [VPIP](/blog/what-is-vpip-in-poker-meaning-formula): VPIP says how many hands someone plays, PFR says how many of those they play by raising.

```
PFR = hands where you raised preflop / hands dealt to you
```

A raise counts whether it is an open raise, a 3-bet, a 4-bet, or an isolation raise over a limper. A call never counts toward PFR.

## A worked example

Out of 1,000 hands you raise before the flop 180 times. Your PFR is 180 / 1,000 = 18%.

## PFR is not the same as RFI

[Raise first in](/blog/raise-first-in-rfi-poker-stat-explained) counts only the first raise in an unopened pot. PFR counts every preflop raise, including 3-bets and 4-bets, so it also reflects how often you re-raise. Two players can have the same PFR with very different opening ranges.

## The VPIP–PFR gap

The most useful thing you can do with PFR is subtract it from VPIP. The gap tells you how many of the hands a player enters are *calls*.

| Style | Example VPIP / PFR | What the gap says |
|---|---|---|
| Tight-aggressive (TAG) | 22 / 18 | Small gap: raises most of what they play |
| Loose-aggressive (LAG) | 30 / 25 | Wide range, but mostly raises |
| Passive "calling station" | 35 / 9 | Huge gap: calls a lot, rarely raises |
| Very tight | 12 / 10 | Few hands, mostly raises |

As a rough guide, many winning 6-max regulars have a gap of only a few points. A gap of ten points or more usually means a player who calls too much before the flop.

## Using it on opponents

- **Small gap, moderate VPIP.** A regular who plays a raise-first style. Their calls are rare, so when they just call, their range is often narrower than it looks.
- **Wide gap.** A passive, loose player. Their raises are very strong, but their calls can be anything. Value bet them thinly after the flop and avoid bluffing them.
- **PFR close to VPIP and VPIP very low.** A nit. Their raises are almost always premium hands.

## Using it on yourself

If you call a lot before the flop and rarely raise, your range is capped and easy to play against. Moving some calls into raises, especially from late position and the blinds when facing a steal, usually improves your results. Look at your [3-bet and squeeze frequency](/blog/squeeze-percentage-poker-stat-explained) to see how often you apply pressure after someone else opens.
