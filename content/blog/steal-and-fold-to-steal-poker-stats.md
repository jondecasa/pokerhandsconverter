## The two sides of the blinds

When the action folds to the cutoff, the button or the small blind, a raise is usually an attempt to **steal** the blinds. Two stats measure it from each side:

- **Steal attempt** (or steal%) is how often you raise first in when you have the chance, from late position.
- **Fold to steal** is how often a player in the blinds folds when someone does it to them.

Definitions vary a little between trackers (some include the small blind as a stealing position, others don't), so check your tracker's definition before comparing numbers.

## Steal attempt

```
steal% = times you raised first in from a late position / times you had the chance
```

A higher steal% means you attack the blinds more often. It should be higher from the button than from the cutoff, because fewer players are left to act behind you. If your steal% is the same from every seat, you probably aren't using position.

## Fold to steal

```
fold to steal = times the blind folded to a steal / times the blind faced a steal
```

Calculated for the small blind and big blind separately. The big blind already has money in the pot and a price to call, so it folds less than the small blind.

## Reading them together

| If the blinds... | Then you can... |
|---|---|
| Fold a lot | Steal more often, with a wider range |
| Defend a lot | Steal less with weak hands, and 3-bet them for value |
| Re-raise often | Tighten your steals or plan to 4-bet. See [4-bet%](/blog/4-bet-percentage-poker-stat-explained) |

The exploit is the point. A wide steal range is profitable against someone who folds too much and costly against someone who fights back.

## Check yourself first

Your own steal stat links to your preflop style. If you play too tight from the button, you leave money on the table. [VPIP, PFR and 3-bet%](/blog/understanding-vpip-pfr-3bet-hud-stats) show the style these numbers sit inside, and [6-max vs full ring](/blog/6max-vs-full-ring-cash-games) explains why steals matter more with fewer players.

## One caution

These are situational stats, so they accumulate slowly. A few dozen opportunities isn't enough to rely on. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) puts numbers on that.
