## What ROI is

**ROI** (return on investment) is the profit you make on tournaments as a percentage of what you spent entering them. It's the tournament equivalent of a cash game win rate, and it's the number most players use to judge whether they beat the field.

```
ROI = (total winnings − total buy-ins and fees) / total buy-ins and fees × 100
```

Count the **fee** as part of what you spent. A $10+$1 tournament costs you $11, not $10.

## A worked example

You play 100 tournaments at $10+$1, so you spend $1,100. Your total prizes come to $1,320.

```
ROI = (1,320 − 1,100) / 1,100 × 100 = 20%
```

You made $220 on $1,100 invested, a 20% return. A negative number means you lost money. Because the fee is built into every entry, a player has to beat the field by more than the fee just to break even.

## ROI vs ITM%

**ITM%** (in the money) is the share of tournaments where you cashed. The two measure different things:

| | ITM% | ROI |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | How often you cash | How much you earn overall |
| Affected by | Finishing in the paid places | The size of the prizes you win |
| Can mislead when | Many small cashes hide losses | A single big win dominates |

A high ITM% with a low ROI means lots of min-cashes and no deep runs. A low ITM% with a high ROI means a few big results carried you.

## Why ROI is so noisy

Tournament results are very top-heavy: most of the prize pool goes to a handful of players. That means one deep run can lift your ROI by a lot, and a long stretch without one can make a good player look like a loser. Your ROI over a few hundred tournaments tells you very little. Many players need **thousands** of tournaments before the number settles. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) covers the same idea for cash games, and the same caution applies here.

The idea of judging results by one headline number also appears in [the All Time Money List](/blog/poker-all-time-money-list-explained), which ranks winnings without subtracting buy-ins.

## Using ROI properly

- **Track profit, not just cashes.** Include every buy-in and fee, and rebuys.
- **Split by buy-in level and format.** A 9-max turbo and a six-max satellite aren't comparable.
- **Don't extrapolate.** A great month doesn't mean a 40% ROI is your true figure.
- **Plan for variance.** Tournament swings are wider than cash game swings, so your buffer should be larger. [Bankroll management for online cash game players](/blog/bankroll-management-online-cash-games) is a starting point, and tournaments need a bigger one.
