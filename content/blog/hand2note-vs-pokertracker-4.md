## Two different philosophies

PokerTracker 4 and Hand2Note both track hands, compute stats, and run a HUD — but they're built around genuinely different ideas of how a player studies. PT4 leans into deep, customizable reporting; Hand2Note leans into fast, visual range and population analysis. Neither is strictly better — they suit different study habits.

## PokerTracker 4: depth and customization

PT4's strength is how far you can slice your database — filtering by very specific spots (3-bet pots from the blinds, particular stack depths, particular positions) and building custom reports around exactly what you're studying. The HUD is fully configurable panel by panel. The tradeoff is a steeper setup curve, especially the first time.

## Hand2Note: visual, range-first analysis

Hand2Note is built around heatmaps and range charts rather than tables of numbers — you see a player's tendencies laid out visually across a 13x13 grid rather than reading a percentage and inferring the range yourself. For players who think in ranges first, this can be a faster way to build reads, both on opponents and on your own play. Its HUD follows the same visual philosophy.

## Population stats and pool-wide tendencies

Both tools let you study population-wide tendencies rather than just individual opponents, useful for exploiting general pool leaks rather than only specific players you've faced before — Hand2Note in particular is often chosen specifically for how accessible this population-level view is.

## Which one fits your study style

If you like building custom reports and drilling into very specific numeric filters, PT4's depth is hard to beat. If you think in ranges and want to *see* a population's tendencies rather than read them off a stat line, Hand2Note's approach tends to click faster. Neither requires you to have picked already — most players form an opinion within a few sessions of trying either on a real database.
