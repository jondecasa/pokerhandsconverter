## What check-raise% measures

A **check-raise** means checking first and then raising when someone bets. **Check-raise%** is the share of the opportunities where a player did it.

```
flop check-raise% = times check-raised the flop / times checked and then faced a bet
```

An opportunity needs three things: the player acted before the bettor, checked, and then faced a bet. If they never get a bet to raise, the hand does not count. Trackers also show the same stat for the turn and the river.

## A worked example

Out of position, you check the flop 50 times and face a bet in 40 of them. You raise in 4. Your flop check-raise% is 4 / 40 = 10%.

## What a normal number looks like

In 6-max cash games flop check-raise% is a small number. Around 7 to 12% is common among regulars. Many weak or passive players sit under 5%, because they rarely check hands with the intention of raising. Treat these figures as a rough guide, since the useful frequency depends on position, board and the opponent.

## What a check-raise is made of

A balanced check-raise range has two parts:

- **Value.** Strong hands such as sets, two pair and top pair with a good kicker on a board where the opponent bets a lot.
- **Draws and bluffs.** Flush draws, open-ended straight draws and some combinations of pair plus draw. They have equity if called, and fold out better hands when the opponent gives up.

A player whose range is mostly value is easy to read, and a player with many bluffs is hard to play against.

## Reading a high or a low number

- **Very low check-raise%.** When this player raises, believe it: their range is mostly the nuts. You can fold more marginal hands, and you can also bet freely, because they rarely punish bets.
- **Very high check-raise%.** This player is aggressive and often bluffing. Do not fold your medium-strength hands too easily, and consider calling the raise more often. Against them, a smaller flop bet can also be an inexpensive way to get information.
- **A check-raise that appears only on certain boards.** If it comes up mostly on boards with many draws, it is probably a draw-heavy range.

## Pair it with fold to check-raise

The mirror stat is **fold to check-raise**: how often a player folds after betting and facing a check-raise. If an opponent has a high fold to c-bet and a high fold to check-raise, you can attack both ends. Read it with [fold to c-bet](/blog/fold-to-cbet-poker-stat-explained) and [c-bet%](/blog/cbet-percentage-poker-stat-explained).

## Using it on yourself

If your check-raise% is almost zero, you may be giving up a powerful weapon, and you are probably calling too often with the hands that want to build a pot or protect against draws. If it is very high, make sure the extra check-raises are backed by hands with equity. Compare it with your [aggression factor](/blog/aggression-factor-poker-stat-explained) to check that your aggression is not all in one street.

## One caution

Check-raising is a rare action, so the sample is small. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) gives a feel for how much a number built on a few raises can move.
