## The structural difference

6-max tables seat up to six players; full ring seats up to nine or ten. That single difference — three or four fewer players — cascades into almost everything else that changes between the two formats.

## Ranges get wider

With fewer players to act behind you, hands that are unplayable in early position at a full ring table become reasonable opens in 6-max — there's simply less chance someone behind you wakes up with a monster. Preflop ranges widen across the board, most noticeably from the earlier betting positions, which barely exist in the same way at a 6-max table.

## "Normal" HUD numbers shift

Because ranges are structurally wider, a VPIP that would look loose at a full ring table can be completely standard at 6-max — see [understanding VPIP, PFR and 3-bet%](/blog/understanding-vpip-pfr-3bet-hud-stats) if those stats aren't familiar yet. This is exactly why HUD profiles are usually set up per table size: reading a 6-max-calibrated read onto a full ring table (or the reverse) will systematically mislead you about who's playing loose or tight.

## More hands per hour, faster variance

Fewer players means more hands dealt per hour at 6-max, all else equal — more hands means faster sample accumulation, which is good for statistical confidence, but it also means variance compounds faster in real time. A losing stretch that would take a week to unfold at full ring can happen in a couple of sessions at 6-max. See [bankroll management for online cash game players](/blog/bankroll-management-online-cash-games) for how that affects buy-in sizing.

## Postflop dynamics change too

More multiway pots are typical at full ring, since more players are seeing flops with wider effective ranges behind them; 6-max skews toward more heads-up and three-way pots. Multiway pots reward different postflop decisions — value bets that are fine heads-up can be thin multiway, and bluffs that work against one opponent often don't against two or three.

## Which should you play?

Neither format is objectively better — it's a genuine preference between faster-paced, wider-range 6-max play and the more multiway, position-sensitive game at full ring. Many players settle into one as their primary format simply because switching means re-calibrating what "normal" looks like on their HUD and in their own head, which takes real reps either way. If you play both, keep separate HUD profiles for each rather than one blended view.
