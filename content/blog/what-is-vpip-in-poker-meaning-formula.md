## VPIP meaning

**VPIP** stands for *voluntarily put money in pot*. It is the percentage of hands in which a player chose to put chips into the pot before the flop, either by calling or by raising. It is the most common HUD stat because it answers a simple question: how many hands does this player play?

```
VPIP = hands where you voluntarily put money in preflop / hands dealt to you
```

## What counts and what does not

- **Counts:** calling a raise, raising, limping, and calling to complete the small blind.
- **Does not count:** the blinds you are forced to post, and checking in the big blind when nobody raised. Neither is a choice.
- **A fold does not count,** and neither does a hand where you were in the blinds and folded to a raise.

Because the forced blinds are excluded, VPIP measures how many hands you *chose* to play, not how many you saw.

## A worked example

You are dealt 1,000 hands. You limp, call or raise before the flop in 230 of them, and you fold or check your big blind in the other 770. Your VPIP is 230 / 1,000 = 23%.

## What a good VPIP looks like

There is no single correct number, because the right VPIP depends on the table size and how you play. As a rough guide:

- **6-max cash games:** many winning regulars sit somewhere around the low to high twenties.
- **Full ring (9-handed):** the numbers are tighter, often mid-teens to around twenty.
- **Recreational players:** often well above thirty, because they play many hands for fun.

These are directions, not targets. Our post on [6-max versus full ring](/blog/6max-vs-full-ring-cash-games) explains why the table size changes the right range so much, and your [raise-first-in percentage by position](/blog/raise-first-in-rfi-poker-stat-explained) shows how wide you open from each seat.

## Reading a high or low VPIP

- **Low VPIP (for example under 15% at 6-max).** A tight player. When they enter a pot they usually have a hand, so respect their raises and steal their blinds.
- **Medium VPIP.** The range of most solid regulars. Read it together with PFR to see whether they are aggressive.
- **High VPIP (30% and up).** A loose player who plays many weak hands. Value bet thinner against them and avoid bluffing a player who calls too much.

VPIP alone cannot tell you whether a player is aggressive or passive. For that, compare it with their [PFR and the gap between the two](/blog/what-is-pfr-in-poker-vpip-pfr-gap).

## Using VPIP on yourself

If your VPIP is much higher than you intend, you are probably playing too many weak hands out of position. Break it down by seat before you change anything: the same hand can be a clear fold under the gun and a clear raise on the button. For the full set of HUD basics, see [understanding VPIP, PFR and 3-bet](/blog/understanding-vpip-pfr-3bet-hud-stats).

## One caution

VPIP settles faster than most stats, but a few hundred hands can still mislead. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) shows where it starts to stabilise.
