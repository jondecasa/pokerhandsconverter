## Downswings are normal

A **downswing** is a stretch of losing results, and every poker player gets them, including winning players. It's variance at work: the maths of a long, high-variance game makes losing stretches certain, not rare.

A sense of scale helps. [Measuring your win rate in bb/100](/blog/bb-per-100-poker-win-rate-explained) shows that after 10,000 hands your true win rate can sit anywhere in a very wide range. That means a decent player can be well below break-even over a stretch of 10,000 hands without doing anything wrong.

## What tilt is

**Tilt** is when emotion takes over your decisions after a bad result. It looks like:

- Calling or raising with hands you'd normally fold.
- Chasing losses by moving up in stakes or playing longer.
- Playing faster, with less thought.
- Blaming luck, opponents or the site instead of reviewing.

Tilt turns a normal downswing into a self-made one, because the extra mistakes cost real money on top of the bad luck.

## Spot it early

Common warning signs: you're annoyed after a loss, you want to "get it back", you're making plays you'd flag in a review, or you've played longer than you planned. Knowing your own signs is half the work.

## Practical rules that help

- **Set a stop-loss.** Decide before you sit down how many buy-ins you'll lose in a session before you stop, and stick to it.
- **Set a time limit,** and take breaks. Fatigue feeds tilt.
- **Don't move up to recover.** Stay at the stakes your bankroll supports. [Bankroll management for online cash game players](/blog/bankroll-management-online-cash-games) gives a framework in buy-ins.
- **Review decisions, not results.** [How to review your hands without a HUD](/blog/review-poker-hands-without-a-hud) is a routine that keeps you focused on play.
- **Look at big samples.** One bad week says little. [How many hands you need before your stats are reliable](/blog/sample-size-reliable-poker-stats) is a useful reminder.
- **Move down if you need to.** A bankroll that has shrunk is a reason to play lower for a while, not a failure.

## When to stop playing for the day

Stop when you can't think clearly: when you feel angry or desperate, or when you're playing to avoid a loss rather than to make good decisions. Closing the table costs nothing compared with a tilted session.

## A word on playing for money

Poker involves real money and real risk. If gambling is causing you stress, financial trouble or harm to your life, talk to someone you trust and consider a support service in your country.
